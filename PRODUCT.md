# Product

## Register

product

*(The Electron app is the primary surface. Work under `site/` — the marketing landing page — runs in the **brand** register.)*

## Users

People who live at a computer all day — writers, designers, and especially developers who run AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) in terminals. They work long, often late, often alone. Context: the cat is permanently on their screen, on top of editors and terminals, so every surface it shows competes with real work for attention. The job to be done: capture thoughts the moment they occur (voice), never lose a commitment (reminders that follow up), know what their agents are doing without alt-tabbing — and feel a little less alone while doing it.

## Product Purpose

Miru is a desktop pixel cat that is also an ambient AI interface. She reacts to typing, scrolling, and petting; she builds a real relationship over weeks (bond levels, morning greetings, streaks with weekend grace, overnight gifts). She is useful: spoken utterances become to-dos, notes, quick answers, or commands typed into a live agent terminal — always behind confirmation chips, always undoable. She watches AI agent sessions and relays their questions. Her look is a swappable sprite pack — three ship built in, and anyone can draw their own as pure data. Success looks like: the cat earns permanent screen residency because she gives more attention than she takes.

## Brand Personality

Calm · premium · minimal. The voice of a quiet, well-made object — a Japanese stationery catalog, not a SaaS pitch. Sincere about the relationship mechanics (never ironic about affection). Understated confidence: state what she does plainly and let the living creature carry the delight. Emotional goals: trust, warmth, calm.

## Anti-references

- SaaS gradient slop: purple-to-blue gradients, floating glass cards, "supercharge your workflow" copy.
- Corporate AI hype: rocket-ship metaphors, "10x" claims, badge walls.
- Retro-arcade CRT nostalgia: scanlines, neon green, 8-bit cosplay chrome.
- Saccharine kawaii overload: pastel-pink everything, comic-sans energy, infantilizing copy.
- Loudness in general: autoplaying carousels, confetti outside the product itself, jokes that beg.

## Design Principles

1. **The cat is the loud one.** Pages and panels stay quiet so the small living creature reads as alive. Pixel art belongs to the cat and her speech bubbles only; everything else is modern, restrained typography.
2. **Show the product doing the thing.** Prefer the real engine (live canvas, faithful UI recreations) over illustration, stock, or metaphor. Nothing on a marketing surface should be fake-able in the product.
3. **Ask before acting; undo after.** Every surface that commits something confirms first and offers a way back. Marketing must never promise behavior the product wouldn't dare.
4. **Never guilt.** Absence is met with joyful reunion, not shame. Copy about streaks, follow-ups, and reminders keeps this tone everywhere.
5. **Earned attention.** Interruptions, motion, and color are spent like money: one accent moment per fold, celebrations tied to real events.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast minimums everywhere (body ≥4.5:1). Full keyboard paths on web surfaces. `prefers-reduced-motion` honored: ambient cat animation reduces to gentle blinking; page reveals become fades/instant. The product itself requires macOS microphone + accessibility permissions — always explained in plain language before requested.
